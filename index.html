About me

A builder who likes the whole stack: interfaces, models, hardware and the messy text in between.

I'm Hanniel Vinu A, a developer from India who loves knowing what makes a computer tick, and then building on top of it. I work across full-stack development, UI/UX, data science and AI/ML, and embedded systems, where I've built multiple award-winning projects.

Natural language processing is where those threads meet. My flagship project, Semmai, checks that an AI's action still means what a Tamil speaker asked. Most of the people my projects serve don't write in tidy English: they write "salary aane pe kar dunga bhai", they speak Tamil or Kannada into a phone, they mix scripts in one sentence. My NLP work is about understanding that language honestly: transparent baselines first, held-out evaluation, and a person in the loop whenever the stakes are high.

★ Flagship · Semantic verification for Tamil LLM agents

Semmai செம்மை

You ask a database a question in Tamil. An AI translates it and writes SQL. Before that SQL runs, Semmai checks that it still means what you asked. If one small word was lost along the way, the query is blocked and the AI is asked to try again.

"Correct syntax does not always mean correct intent."
98.0%
blind Tamil requests, exact meaning (first run)
97.0%
SQL verification accuracy, blind English set
0.0%
wrong SQL let through (false acceptance)
  • Tamil semantic parser: a suffix-aware lexicon that reads case endings and postpositions (2022க்கு பிறகு → after 2022), numbers like 10 lakh, and negation (தவிர → except).
  • Universal Semantic Intent Graph (USIG): the request and the generated SQL are both compiled into the same language-independent "skeleton of meaning".
  • Proof engine: canonicalises equivalent forms (age >= 61 ≡ age > 60), then checks 8 proof obligations with 18 finding types, each explained in plain language.
  • Multilingual tool calls: a character n-gram TF-IDF + logistic regression intent model across 6 languages including Tamil and Hindi, with 93.9% accuracy.
  • Pluggable models: IndicTrans2 (Tamil→English), Qwen2.5-Coder via Ollama, or any OpenAI-compatible API. Semmai's own understanding stays independent, which is why it can catch their mistakes.

Code-mixed intent & date extraction · Razorpay AI Buildathon 2026

Dobaara

Recovers failed UPI AutoPay and eNACH debits. Its NLP core reads what customers reply, in English, Hindi or Hinglish, and turns it into an intent and a promised payment date.

0.932
macro-F1, held-out
93.3%
promise-date exact match
7
intent classes
  • Keyword / regex lexicon written before any data existed, so it couldn't overfit to the test set; LLM parser must beat it to ship.
  • Date normalisation of phrases like "5 tarik ko", "kal tak", "mahine ke end mein" into calendar dates.
  • Gold labels as rules (dom:5, +1, eom) so human judgement is separate from calendar arithmetic.
  • Parser error in the loop: the simulator injects the measured confusion matrix, so mistakes cost money in the results.
"Treat the reply scores as a smoke test." The 180 replies were written by me, so I report that limit up front.

Multilingual speech + entity extraction · Paytm Build for India AI Hackathon 2026

MoneyTrace

An autonomous emergency teammate for UPI fraud victims. It listens to a victim's report in their own language, extracts the financial entities that matter, and links them into a graph that exposes mule-account rings.

  • Speech-to-text with Sarvam AI for Hindi, Tamil, Kannada and English voice reports.
  • Information extraction with Gemini: transaction IDs, UPI handles, amounts, red flags and a severity call, packaged as sealed evidence.
  • Entity triples → knowledge graph with Cognee (add → cognify → search) to connect scammer infrastructure across victims.
  • Calm, step-by-step guidance generated for the victim in real time over server-sent events.

Barrier classification in the patient's language · Health-a-thon 2026

Setu

An assistive follow-up agent that helps cancer care teams find patients who dropped out of treatment, and understand why from what the patient or caregiver says.

  • Closed-label text classification into 8 non-clinical barriers: money, paperwork, travel, lodging, family, felt better, fear, unaware.
  • English, Hindi and Hinglish keyword rules with word-boundary control (dar$ ≠ dard) and Devanagari substring matching.
  • Safety first: an emergency-card check always runs, with reviewed exclusions ("blood test" is not bleeding). Ambiguous matches fail safe.
  • Never clinical: symptom words are logged and routed to a nurse, never answered by the model.

In progress · Cross-lingual meaning comparison

Lost in translation? Bible translation fidelity

Work in progress

The Bible was first written in Biblical Hebrew (with some Aramaic) and Koine Greek. Most people read it in translation, sometimes a translation of a translation. Along the way, meaning shifts: two different words collapse into one, a word with a wide meaning is narrowed, tense and aspect are lost, and phrases are added or dropped.

This project compares each translation verse by verse against the original-language text and flags where the meaning differs, what kind of difference it is, and how big it is, across languages such as English, Tamil and Hindi.

Planned pipeline
01
Parallel corpusHebrew / Aramaic / Greek source texts and translations, aligned by book · chapter · verse
02
Word alignmentLink each source word to its translated words using lexicon numbering (Strong's) and statistical aligners
03
Meaning comparisonCross-lingual sentence embeddings for verse-level drift; a source-word sense inventory for word-level shifts
04
Divergence ledgerEach flagged verse labelled: omission, addition, lexical collapse, sense narrowing, tense/aspect loss or interpretive rendering

Same principle as Semmai: compare meaning, not words. Semmai checks a request against an action; this checks a translation against its source.

φιλῶ σε.phileō · "I love you."
⚑ Two different Greek verbs → one English verb, "love". The contrast in the original dialogue disappears.
